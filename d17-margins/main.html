DEPARTMENT OF ENERGY · OFFICE OF SCIENCE · ASCR

Quantum Sensing Initiative

Program Plan v1.3 · September 2024 · Internal — released via FOIA, May 2026

§1 Program Scope

The Quantum Sensing Initiative is a $1.4 billion appropriationP1 directed at quantum-enabled sensing technologies with defense-adjacent applications, allocated over fiscal years 2024 through 2027. The Initiative consolidates prior DOE quantum-sensing investments into a single coordinated program.

Program scope explicitly includes: (i) magnetic-anomaly detection at the quantum noise floor, (ii) cold-atom inertial navigation in GPS-denied environments, (iii) quantum gravimetry for subsurface mapping, and (iv) advanced cryogenic instrumentation supporting (i)–(iii).

§2 Lead Laboratories

The Initiative is coordinated through four DOE national laboratories, which together hold approximately 78% of obligated fundingP2 over the program horizon:

(a) Brookhaven National LaboratoryP3 — atom-trap sensing testbed and prototype development.
(b) Argonne National Laboratory — solid-state nitrogen-vacancy program.
(c) Oak Ridge National Laboratory — cryo-magnetometer prototype facility.
(d) Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory — quantum gravimetry collaboration with USGS.

§3 Procurement Posture

Procurement is anticipated to phase as follows. In FY24–FY25, the program emphasizes testbed instrumentation acquired through lab-direct procurement vehicles. In FY26 Q4, the program anticipates issuing the first set of solicitationsP4 to non-lab vendors for sensor-array prototypes at TRL 4. In FY27, the program anticipates issuing a second tranche of solicitations for integration prototypes at TRL 5–6.

Contract values above $5M are not anticipated to be let to non-lab vendors prior to FY27 Q1, with the exception of incumbent prime-contractor extensions under existing IDIQ vehicles.

§4 Standards Posture

The program participates in IEEE-1xx quantum-sensing measurement-standard negotiations through the Office of Science's standards-engagement office. As of plan-issue date, the IEEE-1xx working group is in active negotiation; consensus on a Phase-1 standard is not anticipated before mid-FY26.
