The DOE quantum-sensing program ($1.4 B)

A living page. Anyone with something to add can add. Attribution is the log.

The DOE quantum-sensing program is a $1.4 billion appropriation from the U.S. Department of Energy, allocated over fiscal years 2024–2027, directed at quantum-enabled sensing technologies with defense-adjacent applications.

It is currently the largest single U.S. federal allocation to a quantum sub-field; see CHIPS-Act framework for comparable scale in semiconductors.

What we know about the four labs

@alex.p · editing
  • Brookhaven National Lab — atom-trap sensing testbed. As of May 2026, hiring up on the loading dock, cryo equipment arriving (added by @sam.a, attribution verified by @rachel.m).
  • Argonne National Lab — solid-state nitrogen-vacancy program.
  • Oak Ridge National Lab — cryo-magnetometer prototype facility.
  • Lawrence Berkeley National Lab — quantum gravimetry collaboration with USGS.

Procurement signal — what to watch

As of 2026-05-22, no DOE quantum-sensing procurement contract above $5M has been let to a non-lab vendor. Three known pending solicitations are tracked to Q4-2026 / Q1-2027.
