Will DOE's $1.4 B quantum-sensing program produce a procurement signal before Q4 2026?

Three years out from the DOE quantum-sensing program ($1.4 B FY24-27), no contract above $5M has been let to a non-lab vendor. Four labs hold 78% of obligations.

What signals would you trust as "the program is real" vs. "the program is rebadged science funding"? Looking for procurement-side evidence specifically, not lab-side science output.

  • Hiring on lab loading-docks (Sam Ahmadi flagged this for Brookhaven today)

  • Pending solicitations: 3 known, Q4-26 / Q1-27 timing

What am I missing? Especially interested in procurement-clerks, contracting officers, defense-industrial-base vendors, freight visibility, even unrelated supply-chain signals that might say something.

Service-side confirmation of Sam's signal. I service Janis dilution refrigerators across the eastern seaboard. Brookhaven added two new service contracts in March 2026 — that is not normal cadence for them. Oak Ridge added one in February. Argonne added one in April. LBL: nothing new yet but they're still under their old contract terms which expire August.

For your "is this real" test: the service contracts are a faster signal than procurement contracts because they're operational, not capital. Capital procurement always lags by 6-12 months.

Contracting-officer perspective: the signal you should watch is RFI volume, not RFP volume. A program with real procurement intent issues 3-8 Requests for Information per quarter in the year before contracts open. RFIs don't bind anyone, but they leak intent to the market.

DOE quantum-sensing RFI count, Q1 2026: 4. Q4 2025: 1. Q3 2025: 0. The slope inflected this quarter.

Combine Tomás's RFI signal + Sam's dock signal + Rachel's service-contract signal and you have a three-leg stool. Three signals from three angles, all turning positive in the same 90-day window. That's not coincidence in my experience.

A bearish counter-signal worth weighting against the above: the ITAR rewrite Clause 4(b) tightens foundational-research carve-outs in a way that makes university-lab participation in defense-adjacent quantum work substantially harder. If the program is real, this either (a) doesn't matter because primes will absorb the work, or (b) really matters and the procurement signal stalls while DDTC rules on edge cases.

The 90-day comment window closes June 17, 2026. University counsel offices haven't engaged yet (I checked 6 of them). If they engage, the signal could slip.
